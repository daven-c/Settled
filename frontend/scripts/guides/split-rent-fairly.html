Option 1: split it equally

Divide the rent by the number of people. It is simple and works well when the rooms are about the same. If one room is clearly better (bigger, private bathroom, more light), equal rent usually leads to quiet resentment.

Option 2: split by room size or value

Give each room a number of shares that reflects how much better it is, then split the rent in proportion. For example, a $2,400 rent where the large room counts as 2 shares and the two smaller rooms count as 1 share each (4 shares in total):

RoomSharesRent
Large room2$1,200.00
Small room1$600.00
Small room1$600.00
Total4$2,400.00

Each share is worth $2,400 / 4 = $600. Choosing the shares is the hard part: ask what each room is worth to the person in it, or use square footage as a starting point and adjust for the bathroom and light.

Option 3: split by income

Some households split in proportion to what each person earns, so the burden feels equal. It works best when everyone is open about income and the household is long term.

Utilities and shared bills

Utilities are usually split equally, because everyone uses the heat, the internet and the lights. Keep them separate from rent so a change in one does not reopen the argument about the other. For bills that vary, record each one as it arrives and settle the balance monthly.

Write it down

Whatever you choose, record the agreement somewhere everyone can see: who pays what share, who pays the landlord, and how and when people pay each other back. Most roommate arguments are about unclear expectations, not the money.

Do it in Settled. Add the rent as an expense, pick who shares it, and choose shares so the bigger room pays more. The amounts update live and it will not let you save a split that does not add up. Utilities go in the same group, and balances carry across everything.
