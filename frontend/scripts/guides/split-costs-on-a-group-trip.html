Agree on the rules before you go

Most money fights on trips come from surprise, not from the amounts. Before anyone books anything, agree on three things:

  • What is shared and what is personal. Lodging, the rental car and groceries are usually shared. A souvenir or an extra drink is usually personal.
  • Who may book and pay for what. It is common for one person to pay for the lodging and another for the car, so nobody is out of pocket for everything.
  • When you will settle up. Usually once, at the end of the trip, not at every meal.

Let people pay for different things, then even it out

Instead of everyone paying their share of every bill, let whoever is nearby pay, record it, and work out the balances later. Here is a weekend for three people with $1,200 of shared costs:

The balances always add up to zero. To settle, only two payments are needed: Jo pays Alex $340 and Sam pays Alex $160. Alex receives $500, which is exactly what Alex is owed. You do not need a payment for every bill.

Handle the uneven costs separately

A group dinner where some people drank and some did not, or an activity only part of the group joined, should not be split evenly. Split those by who took part or by item, and keep the rest even. Mixing the two methods in one trip is fine as long as every cost is recorded in one place.

Keep a record as you go

  • Record each shared cost the same day, with a photo of the receipt for anything large.
  • Include people who join late or leave early only in the costs they were there for.
  • If someone has not signed up for any app, you can still track them by name and settle with them at the end.

Do it in Settled. Create a group for the trip and add each cost as an expense, choosing who paid and who shares it. For a dinner with different orders, use the by-item option. You can attach up to three photos of a receipt to any expense. A friend who has not signed up can be added by name and linked to an account later. The Balances tab shows exactly who pays whom, with the fewest payments.
